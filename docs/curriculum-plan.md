# Curriculum Plan — 12 Weeks

**Kya banega:** 12 weekly lessons + 84 daily practice sessions + printable reference sheets. Aap roz app me 30 minute practice karoge; har hafte ek short lesson us hafte ka core pattern sikhayega. Grammar book ka order nahi — kaam ke patterns pehle.

---

## How the pieces fit

| Piece | What it is | When it is used |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly lesson (HTML) | One short, self-contained lesson teaching the week's core patterns, with interactive drills | Once at the start of the week, then revisited |
| Daily session (app) | 30-minute practice: warm-up, pattern, words, shadowing, scenario, wrap | Every day |
| Reference sheet (HTML) | Printable pattern bank + phrase bank + pronunciation notes for the week | Quick review, before calls |
| Benchmark recording | Same 4 prompts recorded every week | Day 7, compared over 12 weeks |
| Learning record | One paragraph on what the learner can actually do | After evidence, not after coverage |

The app is the daily gym. The weekly lesson is the coach explaining the movement. The reference sheet is the cheat card. The benchmark is the video review.

---

## The daily session

| Stage | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | 2 min | 5 sentences from yesterday; say them, reveal, self-mark |
| Pattern | 6 min | One frame in positive, negative, question form; 6 substitutions from the learner's world |
| Words | 5 min | 5 new items + up to 10 due SRS reviews |
| Listen & shadow | 6 min | 6-line dialogue; repeat, record, compare; one pronunciation focus |
| Real scenario | 9 min | 4 turns; learner responds and records; soft keyword check; model answer after |
| Wrap | 2 min | 3 recalls, speaking time, streak, tomorrow preview |

Short session (15 min): warm-up + pattern + scenario. Words and shadowing roll into the next session.

## Week 1 — Introduce yourself and your work

**Goal:** At the end of the week, introduce yourself, your role, and your company in 60–90 seconds without freezing.

**Core patterns:**

1. I work at ___ as a ___.
2. I'm from ___ / I live in ___.
3. I have ___ years of experience.
4. I build ___ for ___.
5. I'm good at ___ / I'm learning ___.
6. Do you work ___? / What do you do?

**Words:** developer, client, website, project, meeting, role, team, company, experience, responsive.

**Pronunciation focus:** v vs w; th; word stress in "experience"; question intonation.

**Mission:** Send a 60-second English voice note to a friend introducing yourself and your work.

### Week 1, day by day

| Day | Pattern | Words | Scenario | Pronunciation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | I work at ___ as a ___. | developer, client, website, project, meeting | Client asks, "What do you do?" | v / w |
| 2 | I'm from ___ / I live in ___. | hometown, based, currently, team, company | Introducing yourself at the start of a call | th |
| 3 | I have ___ years of experience. | experience, design, development, manage, handle | Client asks about your experience | stress in "experience" |
| 4 | I build ___ for ___. | e-commerce, app, business, solution, responsive | Explaining what Edone Solutions does | s / sh |
| 5 | I'm good at ___ / I'm learning ___. | frontend, backend, SEO, maintenance, testing | Small talk with a colleague | -ng |

| 7 | Review + benchmark | all Week 1 words | 4 benchmark prompts + mission | review |

## Weeks 2–12 at a glance

### Week 2 — Daily routine and small talk

- **Patterns:** I usually ___ at ___ · I don't ___ very often · How was your weekend? · I went to ___ / I stayed ___ · Do you like ___? · What about you?
- **Words:** routine, commute, weekend, family, food, movie, cricket, gym, traffic, sleep.
- **Scenarios:** Monday morning chat · lunch with a colleague · weekend plans · pre-call small talk · friend catch-up · asking about them.
- **Pronunciation:** -s endings; sentence rhythm.
- **Mission:** Ask a colleague one small-talk question in English.

### Week 3 — Asking and clarifying

- **Patterns:** Can you repeat that, please? · What do you mean by ___? · Could you explain ___? · Is it okay if I ___? · Sorry, I didn't catch that. · How do you say ___ in English?
- **Words:** repeat, mean, explain, clarify, example, detail, understand, slowly, spelling, confirm.
- **Scenarios:** client speaks too fast · unclear requirement · bad phone audio · asking for spelling · confirming a deadline · asking a colleague for help.
- **Pronunciation:** question intonation; polite tone.
- **Mission:** Ask one real clarifying question in English.

### Week 4 — Client kickoff call

- **Patterns:** We need ___ · I'd like to ___ · Could you share ___? · Let's ___ · When would you like ___? · I'll send you ___.
- **Words:** requirement, scope, timeline, design, feedback, reference, kickoff, goal, deliverable, access.
- **Scenarios:** kickoff call · asking for logo/content · agreeing a timeline · confirming next steps · summarizing · scheduling the next call.
- **Pronunciation:** contractions (I'd, let's, we'll); linking.
- **Mission:** Record a 5-line kickoff summary as a voice note.

### Week 5 — Project updates and status

- **Patterns:** I've finished ___ · I'm working on ___ · It will take ___ · We are waiting for ___ · I'll send it by ___ · The current status is ___.
- **Words:** progress, update, complete, pending, review, approve, deadline, delay, ready, testing.
- **Scenarios:** daily standup · client status call · delay update · asking for approval · weekend handover · voice-note update.
- **Pronunciation:** -ed endings; numbers and dates.
- **Mission:** Send a 60-second status update in English.

### Week 6 — Explaining technical work simply

- **Patterns:** It's like ___ · This allows you to ___ · The problem is ___ · It means ___ · For example, ___ · You don't need to ___.
- **Words:** hosting, domain, responsive, load, speed, security, backup, plugin, integration, user.
- **Scenarios:** explain hosting to a shop owner · explain why a site is slow · explain SEO simply · explain a bug · explain maintenance · explain a quote.
- **Pronunciation:** syllable stress in technical words.
- **Mission:** Explain one technical thing to a non-technical friend in 60 seconds.

### Week 7 — Calls and messages

- **Patterns:** Can you hear me? · I'm on a call. · Let me share my screen. · I'll call you back. · Please check your email/WhatsApp. · Sorry, the connection is bad.
- **Words:** call, mute, screen, connection, message, email, WhatsApp, voice note, ring, call back.
- **Scenarios:** starting a video call · bad network · sharing a screen · rescheduling · sending a follow-up · ending a call.
- **Pronunciation:** clarity on phone; pausing.
- **Mission:** Make one real call in English, or record a 2-minute mock call.

### Week 8 — In-person meetings

- **Patterns:** Nice to meet you. · Let me show you ___ · Would you like ___? · We can offer ___ · Here is ___ · What do you think?
- **Words:** office, cabin, sample, demo, price, offer, discount, visit, tour, comfortable.
- **Scenarios:** client visits the office · shop visit · product demo · making an offer · small talk · saying goodbye.
- **Pronunciation:** polite intonation; prices and numbers.
- **Mission:** Visit a shop and ask three questions in English, or role-play it.

### Week 9 — Problems, delays, apologies

- **Patterns:** I'm sorry for the delay. · There was an issue with ___ · It's delayed because ___ · We're fixing it. · I'll update you by ___ · Thank you for your patience.
- **Words:** issue, bug, delay, fix, solve, mistake, patience, update, apologize, reason.
- **Scenarios:** missed deadline · bug on the live site · wrong file sent · payment delay · angry client · recovery plan.
- **Pronunciation:** tone in apologies; stress.
- **Mission:** Record a polite delay message.

### Week 10 — Money, quotes, follow-ups

- **Patterns:** The cost is ___ · Could you confirm ___? · We need the payment. · I'll send the invoice. · When can we expect ___? · Is there any update on ___?
- **Words:** cost, price, quote, invoice, payment, advance, balance, GST, discount, confirm.
- **Scenarios:** sharing a quote · asking for advance · payment follow-up · confirming scope · polite negotiation · closing the deal.
- **Pronunciation:** numbers; lakh/crore vs million.
- **Mission:** Record a payment follow-up message.

### Week 11 — Opinions and suggestions

- **Patterns:** I think ___ · In my opinion, ___ · Why don't we ___? · I agree / I'm not sure. · What if we ___? · That sounds good.
- **Words:** opinion, idea, better, option, suggest, agree, disagree, maybe, possible, prefer.
- **Scenarios:** choosing a design · suggesting a timeline · disagreeing politely · recommending a feature · deciding the next step · casual debate.
- **Pronunciation:** contrastive stress; softening.
- **Mission:** Say one opinion in English in a real meeting.

### Week 12 — Fluency and real calls

- **Patterns:** Let me summarize. · The main point is ___ · Before we finish, ___ · I'll follow up with ___ · Could we schedule ___? · Thanks for your time.
- **Words:** summary, action item, follow-up, schedule, decide, next step, owner, deadline, recap, close.
- **Scenarios:** full mock client call (10 turns) · project review · polite negotiation · closing a call · asking for a referral · final benchmark.
- **Pronunciation:** pausing, rhythm, confidence.
- **Mission:** Complete one real client call using five target phrases; record a 2-minute reflection.

## Weekly benchmark (same every week)

Recorded on Day 7, 30 seconds each:

1. Introduce yourself.
2. Describe your work at Edone Solutions.
3. Talk about last weekend.
4. Handle: "Can you do it by Friday?"

Stored locally. Compared Week 1 vs Week 4 vs Week 8 vs Week 12. The goal is to hear less freezing, clearer pronunciation, and fewer Hindi switches.

---

## Progression rules

- **Completion, not calendar.** The next day is the next unfinished day. Missing a day never skips content.
- **No mastery gate.** Move on after completing a day; weak items come back through SRS and warm-ups.
- **One pattern per day.** If a day feels hard, use the 15-minute short session and repeat the day tomorrow.
- **Production is the test.** A pattern is "done" when it appears in a recorded scenario, not when the learner recognises it.
- **Weekly evidence.** The benchmark and one real-world mission are the only hard weekly requirements.

---

## What gets taught, in one sentence

By Week 12 the learner can introduce himself, make small talk, ask for clarification, run a client kickoff, give updates, explain technical work simply, handle calls, meet in person, apologize for delays, follow up on money, give opinions, and close a call — using ~40 reusable patterns and ~500 words/phrases.
